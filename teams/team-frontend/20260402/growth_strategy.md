# Freeweight Growth Strategy

## Part 1

### Channels and Approach

**Channel 1: Direct outreach to Columbia S&C coaches**
We are going directly to the strength and conditioning staff across Columbia's varsity athletic programs. We have warm relationships from being athletes in the program ourselves, which gives us a foot in the door that cold outreach would not.

Approach: Personal emails and in-person conversations with S&C coaches we have existing relationships with. Ask each coach we onboard to introduce us to one coach at another program. Target: 5 coaches onboarded in the first two weeks.

Message: "We built a tool to replace the spreadsheet. It auto-calculates weights per athlete, tracks completions, and lets athletes flag injuries without texting you. We'd love to get your team on it and get your feedback."

**Channel 2: Warm network expansion through rowers**
Our team are all Columbia rowers. We have direct lines to rowers at peer programs (Harvard, Yale, Princeton, Penn) who train under S&C coaches we can get introduced to through them.

Approach: Text teammates and training partners at other schools, ask for an intro to their S&C coach. Target: 3-5 introductions within the first two weeks.

**Channel 3: LinkedIn outreach to collegiate S&C coaches**
For programs where we have no personal connection, we reach out directly via LinkedIn to strength coaches at Ivy League and NESCAC schools.

Approach: Personalized DM referencing their program specifically. Lead with the Columbia proof point. Target: 20 DMs over 5 days, aiming for a 20% response rate.

Message: "Hi [name], I'm a Columbia rower and we built a strength training app that Columbia's S&C coaches are using. It replaces spreadsheet programming with auto-calculated workouts and real-time completion tracking. Would love to get your take on it — happy to give you a free walkthrough."

### What Does Success Look Like

- 10 coaches signed up within two weeks
- At least 3 of those coaches assign a program to at least one athlete
- 20% response rate on LinkedIn outreach
- At least 2 coaches reached through warm network referral rather than direct outreach

## Part 2

### Results by Channel

**Direct outreach to Columbia coaches**
Strongest channel by far. Jaclyn's existing relationships with coaches across the Columbia athletic department drove the majority of our signups. Coaches responded quickly and were willing to try the product because of the personal connection and the fact that we are athletes in the same ecosystem. Conversion from conversation to signup was high.

**Warm network through rowers**
Mixed results. Teammates were willing to make intros but the conversion from intro to actual coach signup was slower than expected. Coaches at other programs were receptive but less urgent without the in-person relationship. A few expressed interest but had not signed up by the reporting deadline.

**LinkedIn outreach**
Lower response rate than expected. Messages that led with the Columbia proof point performed better than messages that led with the product description. Coaches who did respond were curious but wanted a live demo before committing to signing up.

### What Worked and What Didn't

Warm outreach dramatically outperformed cold outreach. Every signup we got came through a personal relationship or a direct introduction. LinkedIn generated interest but not signups within the measurement window. The product sells better in conversation than in a message because coaches want to see it before they commit.

The spreadsheet pain point resonated immediately with every coach we spoke to. That is clearly the right hook.

### What We Are Changing Next

Doubling down on warm outreach and referrals. Every coach we onboard gets asked to introduce us to one coach at another program. We are also adding a live demo step to the LinkedIn flow rather than asking for a signup directly, since that appears to be where cold outreach was stalling.
